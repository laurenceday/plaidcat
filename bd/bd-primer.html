Primer · Business development

Pitching the data layer without overselling it

What to promise borrowers, lenders, analytics partners and Plaid, and the exact places where a well-meant sentence becomes a contract problem. Everything here compresses the seven explainer pages; follow the links when someone pushes back.

The one-paragraph pitch

Wildcat borrowers connect their operating accounts once, through the same Plaid flow they already use elsewhere. Wildcat keeps a permissioned, continuously updated picture of borrower cash flows, computes agreed credit metrics, and hands lenders signed, dated tear-sheets and onchain attestations. Analysts the borrower approves can compute against defined slices under contract. Raw transactions never reach lenders and never go public. Borrowers can see, restrict, and revoke everything.

Promises that hold

To borrowers

One connect, not a monthly document chase. Scoped consent naming exactly what Wildcat sees and who computes on it. Revocation and deletion rights that actually bind, because Plaid's contracts impose them on Wildcat regardless of statute. Better terms as the reward for transparency.

To lenders

Continuous monitoring instead of quarterly PDFs: metrics refreshed from feeds pulled one to four times a day, staleness always visible, coverage always measured. Signed bundles whose formulas are published. A lapsed or degraded feed surfaces as its own signal instead of silence.

To analytics partners

Scoped, audited access to defined slices under a service-provider agreement, with published disclosure thresholds. Their brand on the derived analytics lenders see.

To Plaid

A commercial-lending underwriting and monitoring use case with a clean consent flow, from a team that passes a SOC 2-shaped questionnaire. Plaid already serves Coinbase and Gemini; this is inside their comfort zone if presented as lending, not as "DeFi data".

Sentences that create problems

  • "Wildcat owns the data." It does not. The borrower owns the raw data; Wildcat holds a license tied to a Plaid-approved use case. Say "Wildcat holds and controls access under the borrower's permission." The legal page has the clauses.
  • "We can see all their banking." Treasury portals at large banks are invisible to every aggregator, and trading firms' fiat rails mostly do not link. Coverage by archetype is on the coverage page; quote those numbers, not "everything".
  • "We'll publish borrower dashboards." Public per-borrower metrics need named consent in the borrower's paperwork and a defensible non-reidentifiability position. Never promise a public dashboard before legal signs the consent text.
  • "Real-time". The feed refreshes one to four times a day with a paid on-demand refresh for decision moments. Say "continuous", never "real-time".
  • "It's like a credit bureau." Only for entities. The moment guarantors or sole proprietors enter the picture, consumer reporting law does too. Leave that sentence to counsel.

Objection handling

ObjectionAnswer
Borrower: "You'll see everything we do."Consent names the accounts and the data types; raw rows stay inside Wildcat's perimeter; lenders get ten agreed metrics with the formulas published; revocation is two clicks and deletion is contractual.
Borrower: "Our treasury team can't hand out the owner login."Correct, and the onboarding runbook handles it: the credential owner runs Link once, OAuth keeps the bank's own MFA, and no credentials ever touch Wildcat or Plaid on the OAuth path.
Lender: "Why trust Wildcat's numbers?"Signed bundles, published formulas, coverage and freshness embedded, an upgrade path to enclave-signed computation, and attestations consumable straight from hooks. And the honest limit stated: bank data does not see exchange exposure, so trading-firm monitoring pairs with exchange attestations.
Partner: "We want to hold the data ourselves."No. Partners compute against Wildcat's perimeter as service providers. That boundary is what makes the borrower consent and the Plaid contract work at all.
Anyone: "Didn't Maple/Goldfinch prove this fails?"They proved self-reported documents fail. Continuous pull-based feeds with completeness checks are the fix, and one protocol (3Jane) runs this exact pattern live today. The moat page has the mortality table.

The asks that matter in the Plaid negotiation

  1. Written use-case approval covering retention, derivation, service-provider analytics access, and derived-metric distribution.
  2. A customer-side derived and de-identified data clause mirroring the one Plaid wrote for itself.
  3. The Transactions-for-Business betas: business-account indicator and business categories.
  4. Transactions Refresh and Statements product access.
  5. An SLA or service credits, since the standard MSA has none.
  6. Calendar the renewal: 60-day non-renewal notice, 30-day rate-change notice.
