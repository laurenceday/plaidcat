## 1. DeFi credit precedents for offchain borrower data

### 1.1 Credora — the closest architectural precedent
- **What it was:** privacy-preserving credit ratings for crypto trading firms (originally "X-Margin"). Borrowers (Wintermute-class market makers) submitted financials + live exchange API read-keys; Credora computed real-time credit metrics **inside Intel SGX enclaves**, with cryptographic proofs that data entered encrypted, was computed on only in the specified way, and never leaked. Architecture claim: "no need to trust the entity running the enclaves; anyone can theoretically run these nodes." ([Credora Medium — ZK computation](https://medium.com/credora/zero-knowledge-computation-13f6c4497df7), [Credora — privacy-preserving credit scoring](https://medium.com/credora/the-future-of-privacy-preserving-credit-scoring-872aeb1e41cc), [credora.network/privacy](https://www.credora.network/privacy))
- **Who held data:** Credora, but sealed in TEEs; lenders consumed **derived ratings/scores + borrow capacity numbers**, never raw positions. This is the canonical "borrower-private raw layer → attested derived layer" pattern Wildcat should study.
- **What happened:** trading-firm ratings business never scaled revenue on its own (Alameda famously carried a strong Credora rating into Nov 2022 — the ratings didn't catch fraud-by-omission; same failure mode as M11/Orthogonal). **Sept 2025: acquired by RedStone** (oracle network; backers incl. Coinbase Ventures, S&P, HashKey). ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/09/04/crypto-oracle-firm-redstone-acquires-defi-credit-specialist-credora), [RedStone blog](https://blog.redstone.finance/2025/09/04/redstone-acquires-credora-strategic-expansion-into-risk-ratings/))
- **Credora Network 2026 (relaunch):** "Credora by RedStone" pivoted from *borrower* ratings to **risk ratings of DeFi vaults/strategies delivered as oracle feeds** — live on **Morpho and Spark** since Nov 2025; ratings distributed via RedStone's oracle API so protocols query price + risk in one call. RedStone claims rated Morpho vaults grew ~25% faster than unrated. ([RedStone blog Nov 2025](https://blog.redstone.finance/2025/11/06/redstone-brings-credora-to-market-following-acquisition-introducing-defi-risk-ratings-to-morpho-and-spark/), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/redstone-acquire-credora-debuts-first-132230884.html))
- **Lesson for Wildcat:** the TEE+proofs architecture worked technically and was accepted by trading firms; the *business* only found product-market fit once ratings were repackaged as an **oracle feed consumed by protocols** (distribution problem, not tech problem). Also: ratings from self-reported/API data are blind to hidden liabilities — undisclosed exposure is the killer, which argues for **completeness checks** (§4), not more cryptography.

### 1.2 Maple Finance
- V1 model: pool delegates (Orthogonal, M11 Credit, Maven 11, Celsius…) did offchain underwriting — financial statements under NDA, monthly reporting, no standardized data pipe. Data flow was email/PDF + trust. ([Maple insights](https://maple.finance/insights/yield-generation-underwriting-and-risk-management), [OAK Research overview](https://oakresearch.io/en/reports/protocols/maple-finance-complete-overview-hub-on-chain-institutional-lending))
- **Failure:** Nov–Dec 2022 — Orthogonal Trading told delegate M11 its FTX exposure was $2.5M, then defaulted on $36M (~30% of active loans); Auros missed ~$3M; ~$54M total sour debt; worst pool lost ~80%. Orthogonal was *both* a delegate and a borrower — governance/data conflict. ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/192097/maple-finance-default-orthogonal-trading), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2022/12/05/maple-finance-severs-ties-with-orthogonal-trading-alleging-it-misrepresented-financial-position), [M11 statement](https://medium.com/@M11credit/m11-credit-update-on-orthogonal-trading-9f896c264f8f))
- **Post-mortem pivot:** underwriting brought in-house (Maple Direct), and by 2025 **all loans overcollateralized** (Syrup, launched May 2024; BTC/ETH/SOL at ≥150%). i.e., Maple concluded self-reported offchain data monitoring was not a sufficient basis for uncollateralized crypto lending and retreated to collateral. ([Maple insights](https://maple.finance/insights/yield-generation-underwriting-and-risk-management), [Spark research](https://www.spark.money/research/tokenized-private-credit-onchain))
- **Lesson:** monthly self-reported financials are worthless in a fast crisis. The data layer must be **continuous, pull-based, and hard to misrepresent** (bank/exchange APIs, not PDFs).

### 1.3 Goldfinch — dead
- Emerging-market fintech lending; underwriting by "backers" staking junior capital, borrower data shared in data rooms off-chain. ~$100M originated; widespread defaults (Tugende, Stratos, Lend East et al.), restructurings failed. Goldfinch Prime (Feb 2025 pivot: onchain access to Apollo/Ares/Golub private credit) failed to get traction; **June 2026: governance voted to wind down, ~$56M frozen in legacy loans**, GFI −99.8% from peak. ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/405825/goldfinch-set-shutter-prime-community-vote-backs-wind-down-proposal), [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/goldfinch-shuts-down-credit-platform/), [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/06/23/aave-founder-reacts-as-goldfinch-shuts-down-with-56m-frozen-in-loans/))
- **Lesson:** decentralized "wisdom of backers" underwriting on offchain-opaque borrowers produced adverse selection; no continuous data feed; recovery on offchain EM loans through DAO governance is near-impossible.

### 1.4 Centrifuge — survivor, different lane
- Asset-level tokenization (invoices/SME loans/funds as NFTs with offchain docs anchored via hashes; privacy goal: "transparency down to the individual asset… but not at the cost of revealing private data"). Pivoted upmarket: V3 (Apr 2025, multichain via Wormhole), $230M Anemoy tokenized fund, >$1.3B TVL, now mostly **tokenized funds/treasuries**, not SME credit data plumbing. ([centrifuge.io](https://www.centrifuge.io/), [Gemini explainer](https://www.gemini.com/cryptopedia/centrifuge-crypto-tinlake-tokenization-real-world-assets), [Coinmonks](https://medium.com/coinmonks/defi-weekly-centrifuge-the-protocol-that-brought-real-world-assets-on-chain-ed6a2e37c0b7))
- **Lesson:** per-asset data anchoring (hash onchain, docs in permissioned data room) is a workable privacy pattern; but the "fraud scoring specialists / auditors as pool service providers" marketplace never materialized at scale.

### 1.5 Clearpool / TrueFi / M11–Archblock
- **Clearpool:** alive; $924M cumulative originations, $67M TVL, $10.75M interest paid (as of 2025 review); pivoted to "Credit Vaults 2.0" / PayFi (e.g., OvalFi RLOC vault, transaction-backed short-term financing) + Ozean RWA chain with Hex Trust + Clearpool Prime for regulated counterparties. Underwriting = borrower-published "financial summaries" + lender DD; still no continuous bank-data layer. ([Clearpool 2025 review](https://clearpool.medium.com/clearpool-2025-yearly-review-e053edc64b5a), [Credit Vaults docs](https://docs.clearpool.finance/clearpool/how-it-works/credit-vaults))
- **TrueFi/Archblock:** legacy pools (TfUSDC, Alameda Credit Private, TfBUSD) carry unresolved 2022-era defaults; **Archblock (the company behind TrueFi) went bankrupt** — forum threads on defaulted pools persist into 2025-26. Effectively dead as an origination platform. ([TrueFi forum — defaulted pools & Archblock bankruptcy](https://forum.truefi.io/t/defaulted-loan-pools-and-archblock-bankruptcy/2880))
- **M11 Credit:** wound down delegate activity after Orthogonal; the delegate model itself (small credit teams, self-reported borrower data) is the discredited artifact.

### 1.6 3Jane — the live Plaid+zkTLS precedent (VERIFIED)
- Unsecured USDC credit lines on **Base** (mainnet Nov 2025; ~$31M TVL as of Jan 2026; initially US residents, assets >$150k; USD3/sUSD3 tranche structure). ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/3jane-to-launch-mainnet-in-november-targeting-defi-credit-market-31653), [web3research](https://www.web3researchglobal.com/p/3jane), [whitepaper](https://www.3jane.xyz/pdf/whitepaper.pdf))
- **Confirmed: 3Jane uses Plaid + zkTLS (Reclaim Protocol).** Borrowers link bank accounts via Plaid and CEX accounts; VantageScore 3.0/FICO via Credit Karma; zkTLS proofs (Reclaim) attest thresholds ("balance > $X") without revealing raw data onchain; fused with onchain history (Cred Protocol, Blockchain Bureau scores; Aave liquidations, etc.) into its credit algorithm. Defaults → legally enforceable claims sold to US debt collectors. ([Leviathan News](https://leviathannews.substack.com/p/3jane-lending-protocol-explained), [Gate Learn](https://www.gate.com/learn/articles/quick-read-3jane-unifying-on-chain-and-off-chain-credit-to-enable-0-collateral-loans/7523), [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2172383))
- **Lesson:** the exact pattern Wildcat is considering (Plaid-sourced cash-flow data → derived credit signals → onchain consumption) is live, small, consumer/SME-scale, and its privacy answer is threshold proofs rather than raw-data publication. Nobody has done it at *institutional trading-firm* scale.

### 1.7 Huma Finance
- Income/receivables-backed → pivoted to "PayFi" (Huma 2.0 on Solana, Apr 2025; $4B+ transaction volume claimed within weeks — mostly short-cycle payment financing turnover, not term credit). Uses "transparent, programmable token streams" + receivables for automated underwriting; earlier versions used aggregator-style income signals via "Evaluation Agents" (pluggable underwriting adapters — a good design pattern). Team ~14 people. ([Messari](https://messari.io/report/understanding-huma-finance-a-comprehensive-overview), [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/humafinance/__X0rxOU1S4USLQBGZc7YlFocXWJj6NffIJ9Dnqa0uFro))
- **Lesson:** short-tenor, self-liquidating receivables minimize the monitoring problem instead of solving it; the "Evaluation Agent" adapter interface is worth copying.

### 1.8 Pipe (offchain, relevant)
- Revenue-based financing marketplace that ingested Stripe/banking/accounting APIs to price recurring revenue streams; after 2022 stumbled (leadership exit, reported credit quality issues) and pivoted to **embedded capital-as-a-service** (MCA-style offers inside vertical SaaS, underwritten on payment-processor data). Lesson: continuous cash-flow data underwriting works best when the underwriter **sits in the money flow** (processor/PSP) rather than observing bank accounts from outside — the repayment can then be deducted at source. [LORE — widely reported; no single canonical source fetched this run]
- **Untangled:** tokenizes fintech-originated private credit (invoices/SME); launched on Celo ($13.5M raised), added Vault + "Embedded Yield Layer" (Apr 2025). Niche; data layer = originator-reported + trustee-style oversight. ([CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/untangled-private-credit-launch/), [Bitget dapp page](https://web3.bitget.com/en/dapp/untangled-25689))

### 1.9 Wildcat itself — public state
- Undercollateralized credit lines where **borrowers set all terms** (rate, reserve ratio, withdrawal cycle, lender whitelist); protocol explicitly does **no underwriting**; docs state lenders "must exercise caution and conduct due diligence." Borrowers to date: Wintermute, Hyperithm, Selini, Amber, Keyrock. ~$150M outstanding / $368M originated since launch (Sept 2025); V2 live on Ethereum since Feb 2025; $3.5M seed extension led by Robot Ventures (Sept 2025, $35M post). ([The Block — raise](https://www.theblock.co/post/369453/wildcat-labs-3-5-million-usd-round-robot-ventures), [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/undercollateralized-lending-wildcat), [docs — elevator pitch](https://docs.wildcat.finance/overview/introduction))
- **V2 hooks/policies:** deposit (and other functions) gated behind hooks; access policies include Chainalysis-sanctions-clean, fixed address sets, NFT/SBT possession, or "a credential testifying to off-chain circumstances" — with KYC/KYB via third parties (Netki, Fractal ID, Civic). ([docs — market access via policies/hooks](https://docs.wildcat.finance/using-wildcat/day-to-day-usage/market-access-via-policies-hooks))
- **Implication:** the hooks system is already the natural onchain attachment point for a data layer: a "borrower-is-current-on-reporting / cash-flow-covenant-satisfied" credential is just another hook credential, and staleness rules can gate *borrower* actions (e.g., APR changes, capacity increases) rather than lender deposits.

### 1.10 What worked / what died — pattern summary
| Pattern | Examples | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Self-reported financials + delegate trust | Maple V1, TrueFi, Goldfinch | Died (fraud-by-omission, FTX contagion, EM defaults) |
| Retreat to overcollateralization | Maple Syrup | Alive, but abandons the thesis |
| TEE-computed private ratings, derived-only disclosure | Credora | Tech worked; business survived only as oracle-distributed ratings (RedStone) |
| Continuous API data + threshold proofs, small tickets | 3Jane | Alive, early, consumer/SME |
| Sit in the money flow (self-liquidating) | Huma, Pipe, Clearpool PayFi | Alive; sidesteps monitoring rather than solving it |
| No underwriting, caveat creditor + hooks | Wildcat | Alive and growing; data layer is the missing complement, not a replacement of lender DD |

## 2. Attestation/verification tech, 2026 state

### 2.1 zkTLS family
- **Reclaim Protocol** — furthest along; proxy-witness model; mobile-speed proofs; ~889 community data sources; supports most chains; **proven in production for exactly Wildcat's data class** (3Jane uses it for Plaid-linked bank data, Credit Karma scores). Providers are defined per-website (URL + JSON path/XPath) — attesting a *Plaid API response* is feasible in principle (Plaid's API is TLS+JSON) but the *user-facing* pattern is proving data behind the borrower's own logins (bank portal, Credit Karma); attesting a server-to-server Plaid response requires the proving party to hold the access_token, i.e., borrower-side or Wildcat-side proving. ([Reclaim zkTLS canon](https://blog.reclaimprotocol.org/posts/zktls-canon), [zk in zkTLS](https://blog.reclaimprotocol.org/posts/zk-in-zktls), [Shoal Research](https://www.shoal.gg/p/zktls-verifiable-data-composability))
- **Opacity** — zkTLS as EigenLayer AVS; MPC-notary model, 32 stETH restake + slashing, commit-and-reveal + notary committees against user/notary collusion; $12M seed; used in DePIN (Nosh, Teleport driver verification). Strongest answer to "why trust the notary," heaviest machinery. ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/321160/opacity-network-funding-zk-data-verification), [EigenExplorer](https://dashboard.eigenexplorer.com/avs/0xce06c5fe42d22ff827a519396583fd9f5176e3d3))
- **zkPass** — MPC→hybrid (proxy-witness in production, MPC backup); SDK v2.0 and consumer apps/node network shipping H2 2026; credit-scoring named as a target use case. ([zkPass roadmap](https://docs.zkpass.org/supports/roadmap), [CMC AI digest](https://coinmarketcap.com/cmc-ai/zkpass/latest-updates/))
- **Primus** — zkTLS attestation piped into a zkVM for *computation over* attested private data (attest → compute metric → prove metric) — architecturally the most interesting for a *metrics* pipeline, least battle-tested. ([Primus tutorial repo](https://github.com/primus-labs/zkTLS-tutorial))
- **TLSNotary / Pluto** — TLSNotary: open-source MPC-TLS engine + browser extension; **not independently audited**, "DYOR before production." Pluto productizes it ("5 lines of code", Venmo/Reddit demos). ([tlsnotary.org](https://tlsnotary.org/), [Pluto repos](https://github.com/orgs/pluto/repositories?type=all), [ZK Podcast #368](https://zeroknowledge.fm/podcast/368/))
- **Maturity verdict:** field crossed from papers to SDKs (Devconnect now runs a dedicated zkTLS Day); proxy-mode proofs are production-real for *point-in-time, user-present, threshold-style* claims. What zkTLS is **not** good at in 2026: bulk longitudinal datasets (24 months × thousands of transactions), server-side scheduled pulls without user presence, and complex derived metrics (except via Primus-style zkVM, which is early). ([Reclaim canon](https://blog.reclaimprotocol.org/posts/zktls-canon), [crypto.news explainer](https://crypto.news/what-is-zktls-web-proofs-explained/))
- **Chainlink DECO:** still described in Chainlink's privacy framing as "a zkTLS oracle… attestations of sensitive offchain data," but no production GA; effectively dormant as a product since the 2023-era sandbox — treat as unavailable for a 2026 build. [Partially SOURCED — [Stanford Blockchain Review on DECO](https://review.stanfordblockchain.xyz/p/74-cryptography-research-spotlight); dormancy assessment is inference from absence of any 2025-26 GA announcement.]

### 2.2 TEE-based attestation
- **Intel SGX:** deprecated on client CPUs (11th gen+); persists on Xeon server SKUs; ecosystem momentum has moved to VM-scale TEEs. **Intel TDX:** hardware-isolated trust domains; attestation quote chains to Intel's root (verifiable without trusting the cloud operator). **AWS Nitro Enclaves:** best ecosystem maturity — SDKs, docs, KMS/ACM integration, signed **attestation documents** (PCR measurements + enclave public key, signed by the Nitro hypervisor, chain to AWS root cert); trust root is AWS rather than silicon vendor. Most production blockchain TEE projects target SGX or Nitro. ([DEV — Nitro vs TDX attestation roots](https://dev.to/voltagegpu/aws-nitro-enclaves-vs-intel-tdx-why-attestation-root-matters-for-regulated-workloads-56ib), [hardenedvault on SGX deprecation](https://hardenedvault.net/blog/2022-01-15-sgx-deprecated/), [Intel note](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/support/articles/000088208/software/intel-security-products.html))
- **Fit for Wildcat:** a Nitro Enclave running the metric engine, whose attestation document (image PCRs = pinned open-source metric code) is published, and whose enclave key signs metric reports — this is "Credora architecture, 2026 edition," with AWS-as-trust-root the honest caveat. Verifying Nitro attestation docs onchain is possible (COSE/X.509 parsing; precedents exist) but usually done offchain with the enclave's signing key registered onchain.

### 2.3 Oracle-signed reports, EAS, PoR analogies
- **Chainlink Functions / custom feeds:** DON-signed delivery of API-derived values onchain; works today; trust = DON quorum + whoever holds the API credentials. **Chainlink Proof of Reserve** is the closest live analogy: scheduled attestation of offchain balances (custodian/bank APIs, auditor feeds) published as a feed consumed by minting/risk logic — e.g., real-time stablecoin reserve attestation. A "Proof of Cash-Flow" feed is the same shape. ([Spark — Chainlink PoR for stablecoins](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-real-time-attestation-chainlink), [Chainlink Q1 2026 review](https://chain.link/blog/quarterly-review-q1-2026))
- **EAS (Ethereum Attestation Service):** open-source attestation registry, onchain or offchain attestations against typed schemas; production-scale on Base (Coinbase Verifications: 77k+ verified users; Coinbase One attestations live July 2026). Perfect fit for publishing **derived borrower metrics or covenant-status flags** under a Wildcat schema, revocable, timestamped, consumable by Wildcat hooks. ([attest.org](https://attest.org/), [coinbase/verifications](https://github.com/coinbase/verifications), [base.easscan.org](https://base.easscan.org/), [Quicknode guide](https://www.quicknode.com/guides/ethereum-development/smart-contracts/what-is-ethereum-attestation-service-and-how-to-use-it))
- **Clean rooms:** AWS Clean Rooms (SQL collaboration; **differential privacy GA since Apr 2024**; multi-cloud incl. Snowflake/Athena sources since Dec 2024) and Snowflake Data Clean Rooms (native, Snowpark, DP models) are mature commercial products. The "**DeFiLlama/analyst computes metrics inside Wildcat's clean room**" pattern is *technically* feasible today: Wildcat provisions a collaboration, whitelists aggregate-only queries (or approved query templates) over the normalized layer, analyst never sees row-level data, egress limited to k-anonymous aggregates. Realistic caveats: clean rooms are built for ad-tech-style join/overlap analytics; custom credit metrics fit better as **pre-approved query templates or Wildcat-reviewed SQL** than free-form exploration; and with a handful of borrowers, aggregate outputs still deanonymize (n≈5 borrowers means every "aggregate" is about somebody) — DP noise at that n destroys utility. Clean rooms shine only once borrower count is ~30+. ([AWS DP GA](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/04/aws-clean-room-differential-privacy), [AWS multi-cloud](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/12/aws-clean-rooms-multiple-clouds-data-sources), [Snowflake DCR](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/unlock-insights-with-snowflake-data-clean-rooms/), [Decentriq comparison](https://www.decentriq.com/article/data-clean-rooms-compared))

### 2.4 Tech-choice summary (what to actually use, 2026)
| Claim type | Best 2026 mechanism |
|---|---|
| "Borrower's bank shows ≥ $X" point-in-time, borrower present | zkTLS (Reclaim-style), borrower-side proving |
| Scheduled derived metrics from Plaid pulls | TEE (Nitro Enclave) signing reports, or plain org-signed reports + published methodology |
| Onchain covenant/status flags for hooks | EAS attestations (revocable, timestamped) on Ethereum/Base |
| Continuous feed consumed by contracts | Chainlink PoR-style custom feed (only if contracts must react automatically) |
| Third-party analysts over sensitive data | Clean room (later stage) or "they-send-code-we-run-it" (early stage) |

## 3. Reference architecture — **DESIGN PROPOSAL** (synthesis; not sourced)

### 3.0 Shape
Five layers: **RAW → NORMALIZED → DERIVED → ACCESS → DISTRIBUTION.** Guiding rules: raw is immutable and append-only; every derived number is reproducible from raw (bitemporal); lenders see derived only; borrower consent is a first-class object; everything that leaves the boundary is signed.

### 3.1 Raw layer (immutable capture)
- **Objects:** every Plaid API response persisted verbatim (gzipped JSON) to object storage: `s3://raw/{borrower_id}/{item_id}/{endpoint}/{yyyy-mm-dd}/{request_id}.json.gz`. Endpoints: `/transactions/sync` pages, `/accounts/balance/get`, `/asset_report/get` (+ Plaid-generated PDF), `/identity/get`, `/item/get`, webhooks. Statement PDFs (`/statements/*` or manual upload) archived alongside with SHA-256 manifest.
- **Cursor journal:** `sync_cursor_journal(item_id, cursor_before, cursor_after, request_id, page_no, added, modified, removed, pulled_at)` — append-only. This is the replayable source of truth; Plaid history caps at **24 months / `days_requested` ≤ 730** ([Plaid Transactions docs](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/), [Plaid Transactions intro](https://plaid.com/docs/transactions/)) so the journal is what turns a 24-month window into an ever-growing archive — *the raw layer is the moat mechanism, see §6*.
- **Security:** S3 Object Lock (compliance mode) on raw buckets; KMS envelope encryption, per-borrower data keys; `access_token`s never in the warehouse — vaulted (KMS-encrypted row in a separate store or a tokenization service), referenced by opaque `item_ref`; access to raw requires break-glass with dual control; all Plaid pulls from one egress-pinned ingestion service.
- **Bitemporality:** every record carries `observed_at` (when we pulled) and the provider's own dates; `removed`/`modified` transactions from sync are tombstoned, never deleted — you can reconstruct "what we believed on date D," which matters when a lender disputes a stale attestation.

### 3.2 Normalized layer (canonical schema — proposed DDL sketch)
```sql
borrower(borrower_id PK, legal_name, jurisdiction, onchain_addresses[], status, consent_scope_json, created_at)
institution(institution_id PK, plaid_institution_id, name, country, oauth boolean)
item(item_id PK, borrower_id FK, institution_id FK, item_ref /*vault pointer*/, status ENUM(healthy,login_required,pending_expiration,revoked), last_successful_sync, consent_expires_at)
account(account_id PK, item_id FK, borrower_id FK, plaid_account_id, name, official_name, mask, type, subtype, currency, is_operating boolean /*classified*/, opened_first_seen, closed_last_seen)
transaction(txn_id PK, account_id FK, borrower_id FK, plaid_txn_id, posted_date, authorized_date, amount NUMERIC(18,2) /*sign: + = outflow in Plaid; store normalized signed_amount: + = inflow*/, iso_currency, counterparty_id FK NULL, raw_name, merchant_name, category_primary, category_detailed /*Plaid PFC*/, class ENUM(revenue, intercompany, financing_inflow, sweep, refund, opex, payroll, debt_service, tax, distribution, fee, nsf_fee, other), class_source ENUM(rule, model, vendor, manual), pending boolean, removed_at NULL, observed_at, source ENUM(sync, asset_report, statement_ocr))
balance_snapshot(account_id FK, as_of date, available NUMERIC, current NUMERIC, observed_at)
counterparty(counterparty_id PK, borrower_id FK, canonical_name, type ENUM(customer, supplier, exchange, affiliate, lender, payroll_provider, tax_authority, self), match_keys[])
metric_run(run_id PK, borrower_id, window_start, window_end, code_version /*git sha of metric engine*/, input_manifest_hash, created_at)  -- lineage
freshness(borrower_id, item_id, last_txn_posted, last_sync_at, staleness_hours, coverage_ratio)
```
Key normalization decisions: (a) **signed_amount convention fixed once** (Plaid's positive=debit trips everyone); (b) `class` is the load-bearing column — every metric depends on classification, so `class_source` + manual-override audit trail is mandatory; (c) counterparty resolution table is borrower-scoped first (cross-borrower canonicalization is a later, valuable asset); (d) accounts flagged `is_operating` per §4 coverage test.

### 3.3 Derived layer — metric definitions (exact formulas)
All metrics computed per `metric_run` over window W (default: trailing 12 calendar months of *complete* months), on **posted, non-pending, non-removed** transactions in **operating accounts** unless stated. Let `IN(m)` = Σ signed_amount>0 in month m with class ∈ {revenue}; `IN_gross(m)` = all inflows; `OUT(m)` = Σ outflows excluding class ∈ {intercompany, sweep, distribution}; `NCF(m) = IN(m) − OUT(m)`.

1. **TTM inflows** = Σ over last 12 complete months of `IN(m)` (revenue-classified only). Report alongside `IN_gross` and the **exclusion delta** (gross − revenue) so lenders see how much was filtered as intercompany/financing/sweep — the delta itself is a fraud signal.
2. **Avg monthly operating cash flow** = mean(`NCF(m)`) over W. Edge: months with coverage_ratio < 0.95 (item outage) are excluded and the exclusion is disclosed in the report (`months_used`).
3. **Minimum cash balance** = min over daily reconstructed balance across W of Σ current balances of operating accounts. Daily balance reconstructed from latest `balance_snapshot` anchor − running transaction deltas (Plaid gives point balances, not daily history; reconstruct backwards from anchor). Edge: multi-currency → convert at daily ECB/ frozen month-end rate, disclose basis.
4. **Liquidity trend** = OLS slope of month-end total cash over last 6 months ÷ avg month-end cash (unitless %/month); report with R².
5. **Customer concentration HHI** = Σ_c (share_c)² where share_c = revenue-classified inflows from counterparty c ÷ TTM inflows; counterparties resolved via `counterparty`; unresolved inflows bucketed as `unknown` and **HHI reported with % unresolved** (an HHI over 40%-unresolved inflows is meaningless — publish both numbers).
6. **Recurring obligations** = Σ monthly-equivalent of outflow series detected as recurring: same counterparty ∧ amount within ±10% ∧ period 27–33d (monthly) or 6–8d (weekly), ≥3 occurrences. Classes: debt_service, rent, payroll, subscriptions. Report as $/month + count of series.
7. **NSF/overdraft frequency** = count of transactions with class=nsf_fee (Plaid PFC `BANK_FEES.OVERDRAFT_AND_NSF` + name-regex fallback) per 12 months; plus days-with-negative-available count.
8. **Cash-flow volatility** = stdev(`NCF(m)`)/|mean(`NCF(m)`)| over W (CV); if mean ≈ 0, report stdev in $ and flag. Also inflow volatility separately (revenue stability vs. spend discipline).
9. **Operating-account coverage** = (revenue-classified inflows visible in connected accounts over W) ÷ (borrower-claimed revenue for same period, from rep letter or financials). Target ≥ 0.85. *This is the anti-gaming keystone (§4).*
10. **Period analyzed / data freshness** = window_start, window_end, months_used, per-item `staleness_hours`, `coverage_ratio` = days with successful sync ÷ days in W. Every published metric bundle embeds these — a metric without freshness metadata is treated as stale by consumers (§3.5).

**Named edge cases (classification rules that make or break every number above):**
- **Intercompany transfers:** same-owner account pairs (matched via identity data, mask+name heuristics, and borrower-declared affiliate list) → class=intercompany, excluded from inflows/outflows. Failure to exclude inflates TTM inflows arbitrarily (self-wash). Detection: offsetting amounts ±0-2 days across connected accounts; counterparty names matching borrower/affiliates; round amounts recurring at month boundaries.
- **Sweep accounts:** brokerage/MMF sweeps look like huge symmetric daily flows → class=sweep via symmetric-pair detection (same counterparty, in/out netting ≈ 0 over 7d) and account subtype; excluded from operating cash flow, *included* in cash balance.
- **Credit-line draws masquerading as inflows:** inflows from counterparty type ∈ {lender, exchange} or PFC `TRANSFER_IN.LOANS` → class=financing_inflow, never revenue. Heuristics: round amounts, non-recurring, counterparty name matching known lenders/venues; borrower attestation of debt schedule cross-checked against detected debt_service outflows (an interest payment with no declared facility = red flag).
- **Refunds/chargebacks** net against revenue in month of occurrence; **tax authority** flows classed separately; **exchange transfers** (Kraken/Coinbase/Wintermute-style counterparties) are their own class for crypto-native borrowers — for trading firms, "revenue" via bank rails is often meaningless and the honest product is *cash & flow transparency metrics*, not P&L inference. Design accordingly: metric bundle A (corporates: revenue-centric), bundle B (trading firms: liquidity/flow-centric).

### 3.4 External analytics access layer
Three postures, in order of increasing cost; recommend **starting at (1), moving to (3) only if third-party analytics becomes a product**:
1. **They-send-code-we-run-it** ("reverse clean room"): analyst (e.g., a DeFiLlama-style shop, a rating boutique) submits versioned SQL/Python against the *normalized schema spec*; Wildcat runs it in CI against real data; only aggregate outputs above k-anonymity thresholds (and borrower-consented granularity) are released, with human review. Cheap, auditable, right for n<20 borrowers.
2. **Scoped warehouse views:** per-analyst service accounts on Snowflake/BigQuery with row-level security (borrower allowlist derived from consent objects), column masking (no raw_name, no counterparty names — only anonymized counterparty_ids), query gateway that logs every statement, egress caps (result-row limits, no SELECT * on transaction), and dynamic data masking. Works, but "a view on transactions" leaks more than people think (timing + amounts fingerprint counterparties).
3. **Clean room proper:** AWS Clean Rooms or Snowflake DCR collaboration; Wildcat contributes normalized tables with analysis rules = aggregation-only + custom query templates; optional differential privacy (GA on AWS since 2024). Only meaningful at ~30+ borrowers (small-n aggregates deanonymize; DP noise at small n destroys utility). ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2024/04/aws-clean-room-differential-privacy), [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/unlock-insights-with-snowflake-data-clean-rooms/))
Cross-cutting: immutable query audit log (who/what/when/rows-touched), per-borrower consent scopes enforced in the view layer (a borrower can consent to lender-tearsheets but not cross-borrower benchmarking), and contractual + technical egress control (no UDF network access, no external stages).

### 3.5 Lender distribution layer
- **Signed JSON attestation bundle** per borrower per run: `{borrower_id, metrics{...}, window, months_used, coverage_ratio, staleness_hours, code_version, input_manifest_hash, issued_at, expires_at}` signed by (a) Wildcat org key, and later (b) a Nitro Enclave key whose attestation document + pinned open-source metric code are published (Credora pattern, §2.2) — upgrade path from "trust Wildcat" to "trust the enclave + code."
- **PDF tear-sheet**: human-readable rendering of the same bundle (hash of JSON printed on the PDF), for credit-committee consumption.
- **EAS onchain attestations** (Ethereum mainnet, matching Wildcat deployment): schema `WildcatBorrowerHealth(bytes32 borrowerRef, uint64 windowEnd, uint32 coverageBps, int64 avgMonthlyOCF_cents, uint32 hhiBps, uint16 nsfCount, uint64 minCash_cents, uint8 tier, uint64 expiry)` — or, more conservatively, only **covenant-status booleans/tiers** onchain and full metrics offchain-signed. Attestations are revocable (borrower revokes consent → revoke attestation) and expire; **Wildcat hooks consume them**: e.g., a market's capacity-raise or APR-reduction hook requires a non-expired, non-revoked health attestation. ([attest.org](https://attest.org/), Wildcat hooks: [docs](https://docs.wildcat.finance/using-wildcat/day-to-day-usage/market-access-via-policies-hooks))
- **Freshness/staleness rules:** attestation `expires_at = issued_at + 35 days` (monthly cadence + grace); consumers must treat `coverage_ratio < 0.9` or any `item.status != healthy` lasting > 7 days as **degraded** (published as a flag, not silently); a lapsed attestation is itself a signal and should surface in the lender UI ("borrower reporting lapsed N days").
- **Privacy gradient options for the bundle:** exact values → bucketed tiers → threshold booleans → (later) zkTLS/zk threshold proofs where the borrower proves a claim without Wildcat re-publishing it. Trading firms will push for tiers/thresholds; corporates may accept exact values under lender NDA-gated decryption (encrypt bundle to whitelisted lender keys — the lender whitelist already exists in Wildcat markets).

## 4. Anti-gaming and data quality — **DESIGN PROPOSAL** (threat-model synthesis)

**Attacks:**
1. **Round-tripping / self-wash:** borrower cycles funds through an unconnected affiliate account back into the connected operating account as fake "revenue." Detections: inflow spikes from young/unresolved counterparties; inflow/outflow symmetry to same or related counterparty over 1–30d; revenue inflows with no corresponding operating-expense texture (real revenue co-occurs with payroll/COGS/refunds); benchmark inflow-size distributions (Benford-ish tests on amounts; fake flows are round).
2. **Hiding accounts:** connect only the flattering accounts. This is *the* structural weakness of consent-based bank data — Plaid shows what's linked, not what exists. Mitigations: **operating-account coverage test** (metric #9): reconcile connected inflows to claimed revenue from (audited or reviewed) financials/tax filings; require rep & warranty listing all accounts (legally actionable, cf. 3Jane's legal-enforceability design); scan connected-account transactions for **transfers to/from unlinked self-owned accounts** (counterparty name = borrower's own name/affiliates at another institution = smoking gun); require linking any account that receives > X% of detected transfers.
3. **Timing/window-dressing:** park borrowed cash over month-end snapshots; time revenue recognition into the window. Mitigations: daily reconstructed balances (not month-end only), min-balance metric over *all* days, financing_inflow classification (§3.3), flag balance spikes with < 7-day residence time ("hot money residency" metric).
4. **Classification gaming:** structure transfers to look like customer receipts (rename entities, use PSP intermediaries). Mitigations: PSP payout de-aggregation (Stripe/Adyen payouts should be decomposed via processor linking where possible), counterparty KYC on top-10 inflow sources for large facilities, cross-borrower counterparty graph (the same "customer" paying three Wildcat borrowers who happen to share a beneficial owner is visible only to the platform — a genuine platform-scale advantage, §6).
5. **Item manipulation:** "accidentally" break the Plaid Item before bad weeks. Mitigation: staleness is a first-class published signal (§3.5); repeated outages correlated with volatility get their own flag; webhook-monitored `ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED` timestamps are archived.
**Completeness checks (run every cycle):** connected-inflows ÷ claimed-revenue ≥ threshold; detected debt_service ⊆ declared debt schedule (and vice versa); payroll present if headcount claimed; tax payments consistent with claimed revenue scale; transfer-graph closure (all major transfer counterparties either linked or explained).
**Honest limits:** bank data cannot see exchange balances, prime-broker margin, or offchain derivatives (the exact holes that killed Maple's book in 2022 — Orthogonal's hidden FTX exposure was invisible to any bank feed). For crypto-native borrowers the bank layer must be *combined with* exchange read-API / custodian attestations (Credora's original design) or it monitors the wrong pocket. This should be stated plainly in lender-facing docs.

[LORE — Plaid doesn't publish enterprise pricing.]

Ops design: webhook-driven health dashboard, `PENDING_EXPIRATION` pre-emption, automated re-link emails with update-mode Link, and — because borrowers are counterparties with obligations, not consumers — **contractual cure periods**: re-link within 5 business days or the attestation degrades. Budget 0.25–0.5 FTE ongoing for re-link campaigns + classification-rule maintenance + monthly metric-run review.
- **Build-vs-buy (metric engine):** the classification/metric middle of this stack is a commodity you can buy:
  - **Heron Data** — purpose-built for SMB/MCA cash-flow underwriting from bank data; revenue/recurring-expense detection ([Heron docs — SMB underwriting quickstart](https://docs.herondata.io/use-cases/smb-underwriting)).
  - **Ntropy** — transaction enrichment/categorization API tuned for cash-flow underwriting inputs ([Ntropy — cash-flow underwriting](https://www.ntropy.com/blog/usecase-spotlight-cashflowunderwriting)).
  - **Ocrolus** — statement OCR + income/NSF/cash-flow analytics, feeds loan-origination systems; strongest when borrowers submit PDFs instead of/alongside Plaid ([Ocrolus API flow](https://docs.ocrolus.com/docs/ocrolus-api-flow)).

## 6. Defensibility analysis inputs — and verdict

**The bear case is structural:** any competitor, with the borrower's consent, gets **24 months of the same history in roughly one API call** (Plaid `days_requested=730`; [Plaid docs](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/)). Consent-based bank data is by construction **re-obtainable and non-exclusive**. Borrower switching cost from the data itself ≈ zero; a "data escrow" framing (borrower-owned archive, portable) is actually the honest product posture and CFPB-1033-era open-banking rules push the same direction.

**What does compound:**
1. **Longitudinal depth beyond 24 months.** From month 25, Wildcat's cursor journal contains history no re-link can reproduce (aggregator window caps it). Through-a-full-cycle cash-flow history on the same borrower is unpurchasable. But note: it compounds slowly and only matters if tenor/renewal decisions actually use >24mo of history.
2. **The consent graph.** Standing, contractual, monitored connections to N borrowers — with cure periods, coverage tests, and rep letters attached — is an *operational* asset, not a data asset. It's Plaid-for-this-asset-class plus enforcement teeth. Competitors must re-negotiate each borrower; borrowers who've done the painful account-mapping/classification-override work once won't relish repeating it. Moderate moat; think "switching friction," not "lock-in."
3. **Outcomes joined to cash-flow patterns.** Defaults, near-misses, covenant breaches, cure behavior, and *withdrawal-run dynamics in Wildcat markets* linked to the preceding cash-flow trajectories = genuinely proprietary training data for credit models. Nobody else can assemble the label side (onchain credit outcomes at institutional scale) even if they could re-pull the feature side. This is the same asymmetry that made Credora's ratings worth acquiring (RedStone bought methodology + track record, not raw data), and the asset Maple/Goldfinch never systematized — each blow-up produced lessons but no reusable labeled dataset.
4. **Cross-borrower structures:** counterparty graph across borrowers (shared "customers," shared funding sources — §4's wash-detection), peer benchmarks ("this market maker's liquidity CV is 84th percentile") — visible only from the platform seat, and precisely what clean-room access could later monetize.

**Verdict (honest):** the moat is **not the data** — raw bank history is a commodity re-obtainable by anyone with consent, and pretending otherwise would misprice the whole effort. It is **partly the consent graph** — standing monitored connections with contractual teeth create real but shallow switching friction — and it is **mostly the outcomes dataset**: Wildcat is uniquely positioned to join continuous cash-flow features to onchain credit outcomes (repayment, delinquency, market-run behavior) at the institutional-crypto-borrower frontier where no incumbent (bureau, Plaid, Ocrolus, Credora-by-RedStone) has labels. The strategic implication: design the data layer from day one as a **label factory** — bitemporal raw archive, reproducible metric runs, outcomes tables — because the derived scores are what get consumed (Credora's lesson: distribution as oracle/attestation feeds is where the value realized), while the labeled longitudinal corpus is the only thing a competitor cannot re-pull in one API call.
