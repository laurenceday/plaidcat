Primer · Legal & compliance

The checklist

Everything counsel needs to paper before the first borrower clicks Connect, in workable order. The clause analysis and citations live on the data-rights page and in research stream D. This run is research, not legal advice; quoted clauses were captured through search extraction and want re-verification against live pages.

1 · The Plaid order form

  • Written use-case approval that names, in words: retention of pulled data, internal derivation of credit metrics, analytics access by service providers, and distribution of derived, entity-level metrics to lenders (and publicly, if that is ever wanted). Name Assets as the underwriting artifact and Transactions as the monitoring feed: the Financial Insights docs bar Transactions from "credit or underwriting decisioning", and pre-agreeing the framing defuses that clause.
  • A customer-side derived-data and de-identified-data clause. Plaid's standard paper grants that right to Plaid alone, in its sole discretion, and grants the customer nothing symmetric. This is the negotiation's center of gravity.
  • SLA or service credits; the standard MSA has neither.
  • Diary the mechanics: 60-day non-renewal notice, 30-day rate-change notice at renewal, 10-day breach cure, 30-day post-termination download window.

2 · The borrower consent artifact

  • Clear and conspicuous, per the MSA's Express Consent standard. Names: the data categories pulled, the retention period or criteria, the derivation of metrics, the recipient categories (analytics service providers, auditors, lenders receiving derived metrics), and any publication.
  • Signed by a person with authority to bind the entity and access the accounts: an officer's certificate plus a rep and warranty that all operating accounts are disclosed and the connecting login is the account holder's.
  • Re-consent on material scope change. Revocation honored promptly; deletion on request is contractual under Plaid's flow-downs whatever statute says.
  • Loan-agreement companions: the re-link obligation with a cure period, the monitorable-operating-account covenant, and the coverage representation the anti-gaming tests check against.

3 · Intake screening rules

  • Entities only. No sole proprietorships, no personal accounts, no personal guarantor cash-flow analytics. Any of those pulls the model toward consumer reporting and GLBA territory; if lenders demand guarantor analytics, route them through an existing consumer reporting agency or accept CRA status as a deliberate decision.
  • Tokenize person-identifying strings (payroll lines, counterparty names) at ingestion. It shrinks CCPA exposure for California individuals and is the difference between manageable and miserable under GDPR for any EU/UK borrower.
  • EU/UK borrowers get their own consent flow: controller obligations, lawful-basis analysis for embedded employee data, purpose limitation under open-banking rules, and no public per-borrower metrics.

4 · Papering the third parties

  • Analytics partners sign service-provider agreements: process only for Wildcat, no independent use, deletion, security, Plaid restrictions flowed down. Strike any vendor clause claiming ownership of data derived from Wildcat's data; that grab is market-standard and negotiable.
  • Lenders receiving anything recognizably Plaid-sourced sign flow-down terms. Genuinely derived entity-level metrics argue lighter treatment; read the paper broadly anyway.
  • Auditors go through Plaid's own rails (Audit Copy, Relay) where possible, so consent and audit trails live inside Plaid.

5 · The watchlist

CFPB 1033 reconsideration (at OIRA since Aug 2026)Consumer-only either way, but its consent-hygiene architecture is the market template, and a fee-permissive rule entrenches bank-side charging.
New York Financial Data Rights Act (proposed)Would cover small-business data with per-violation penalties: the first statute that would actually touch this model.
Bank data-access agreementsNo statute obliges banks to serve business-account data; JPMorgan's paid regime is live and spreading. Supply continuity is the long-run threat.
Plaid's hidden cash-flow report productIf it ships, its terms may package exactly what Wildcat assembles by hand, with different data-rights language.
LEI requirementPlaid requests a Legal Entity Identifier from OAuth-connecting customers under the 1033 rule; enforcement is paused pending the rewrite, but obtaining one early is cheap.
Item revocation asymmetriesBank-side revocation may never signal; removal may leave stale bank-side grants. The borrower offboarding runbook should cover both ends.
