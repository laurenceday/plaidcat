## 1. Plaid's contractual framework for customers (integrators) — CONTRACT TEXT

Plaid's customer-facing framework as of 2025–2026 consists of: (a) the **Plaid Master Services Agreement / Plaid Customer Agreement** (negotiated or clickwrap; a full copy of Plaid's standard MSA is public because a Plaid customer, **Live Oak Financial LLC**, filed it as an exhibit to a Form 1-A/1-A/A in 2025 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2069448/000206944825000001/Plaid_msa.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2069448/000206944825000057/plaid_msa.htm; mirror: https://assets.disclosurequest.com/Archives/edgar/data/2069448/000206944825000056/plaid_msa.htm); (b) the **Developer Policy** (https://plaid.com/developer-policy/), incorporated into all customer agreements; (c) **Terms of Use / flow-down terms** (https://plaid.com/legal/terms-of-use/; a customer-hosted mirror of Plaid flow-down terms exists at https://www.bectran.com/terms-and-conditions/plaid-terms-of-use); (d) the **End User Privacy Policy** and **End User Services Agreement** (see §2). Index page: https://plaid.com/legal/.

### 1.1 Who owns the data?
- **[Q]** MSA: Plaid "reserves and retains all right, title, and interest in the Services and related Output (**except for raw End User data, which belongs to the End User**), software, products, and other intellectual property created or provided by Plaid." (Plaid MSA, SEC exhibit, URL above.)
- Net position: **the end user owns raw End User Data; Plaid claims IP in the Services and "Output"; the customer owns neither raw data nor Output** — the customer gets a limited use right. This is the single most important structural fact: Wildcat is a licensee of everything it receives via Plaid, not an owner.

### 1.2 Permitted use — tied to the approved application/use case
- **[Q]** "The 'Output' is defined as the End User information and data provided via the Services, which Client must use **solely in their Client Application for the use case permitted by Plaid in writing**." (Plaid MSA, SEC exhibit.)
- **[Q]** Older Plaid Service Agreement (mirrored at https://gist.github.com/kirillzubovsky/4937e0c14c843d11e2399c83d1e47052): "All End User information and data provided via the Service will be used by the Client **solely for the purpose of providing the Client Application to the applicable End User** (and it will not be otherwise used or distributed)."
- Implication: any use beyond the Plaid-approved use case (e.g., building a market-data product, selling benchmarks) requires Plaid's written approval, independent of end-user consent.

### 1.3 Sharing with third parties / service providers / resale
- **[Q]** MSA "Data Disclosure": "Client will not **disclose, transfer, syndicate or distribute End User Data to any third party (including its Permitted Service Providers)** except in each case **with the End User's Express Consent** and in accordance with applicable laws."
- **[Q]** MSA defines Permitted Service Providers: "Client may permit its employees, agents, contractors and service providers to access the Services and Output on Client's behalf (such parties, the 'Permitted Service Providers')." Even these require Express Consent for disclosure of End User Data (per the clause above).
- **[Q]** MSA general restriction: "Unless Plaid specifically agrees in writing, clients cannot make the Services or Output available to, or use the Services or Output for the benefit of, anyone other than Client or End Users, and **cannot sell, resell, license, sublicense, distribute, rent or lease any Services or Output to any third party**."
- **[Q]** Developer Policy: developers agree not to "**sell or rent End User Data to marketers or any other third party**." (https://plaid.com/developer-policy/)
- **Flow-down regime for onward sharing:** Plaid's Terms of Use contain "End Client Flow Down Terms" for a defined class of downstream recipients ("**Secondary Investors**" — terminology from lending/secondary-market use cases): **[Q]** "End Client must enter into **legally binding written agreements with each Secondary Investor** that are consistent with... the End Client Flow Down Terms, including restrictions"; "End Client remains responsible for Secondary Investors' compliance... including terms relating to use of **Plaid-Provided Data or Shared Data**"; "End Client is fully liable for... any acts or omissions of Secondary Investors." (https://plaid.com/legal/terms-of-use/; mirror https://www.bectran.com/terms-and-conditions/plaid-terms-of-use.) So Plaid's own paper *contemplates* onward sharing — but only via written flow-down contracts + Express Consent, with the customer strictly liable.

### 1.4 Retention & post-termination
- **[Q]** Developer Policy / ToU: "End Client will **promptly Delete any FI Data upon request by the applicable End User**; provided that End Client may retain copies of FI Data solely to the extent required by applicable laws."
- Developer Policy: developers "must not collect and store end users' bank credentials and/or End User Data **other than as required to access or use the Services, as authorized by the end user, as permitted by Plaid, and as permitted under applicable law**." (https://plaid.com/developer-policy/)
- Product-specific termination clauses exist (e.g., **[Q]** IDV: on termination "Plaid will delete all Client Data stored on Plaid's servers relating to such Order... Client will have thirty (30) days to download"; Signal: "Except for Client's right to use the Signal Service to generate the Signal Output, all provisions of the applicable Exhibit will survive"). **UNVERIFIED:** whether the current standard MSA obliges the *customer* to delete previously-retrieved End User Data on termination of the Plaid relationship — no clause to that effect surfaced; the retrieved-data retention right appears to survive subject to end-user consent scope and deletion requests. Treat as open until the full MSA text is read.
- Plaid side: **[Q]** "After Account deactivation, Plaid will deprovision access to all End User Data associated with the integration," while Plaid itself may retain data as permitted by law (Developer Policy).

### 1.5 Deletion on end-user request
- Contractually mandatory for the customer (quote in §1.4). Plaid also publicly states it "**contractually requires third parties to delete consumer data upon the consumer's request**, to protect consumer data with an information security program..., to comply with laws... (such as the GLBA Safeguards Rule), and to avoid engaging in the sale or rental of consumer data for things like marketing or behavioral targeting." (Plaid RFI response to CFPB, https://downloads.regulations.gov/CFPB-2023-0052-0917/attachment_1.pdf; https://plaid.com/blog/open-finance-trust-security/)

### 1.6 Audit rights — they run in BOTH directions and reach the FIs
- **[Q]** ToU/flow-down: "Toward assessing End Client's material compliance with FI Data requirements, End Client will promptly provide all reasonably necessary information and cooperation requested by **Plaid, an FI, or any entity with examination, supervision, or other legal or regulatory authority over Plaid or an FI**." If documentation is insufficient "in Plaid's reasonable discretion," **[Q]** "End Client will submit to a **third-party audit** by a firm selected by End Client from a list of audit firms reasonably approved by Plaid." "**Plaid and FIs may also conduct technical or operational assessments** of End Client" (advance notice; max 1x/year unless legally required). (https://plaid.com/legal/terms-of-use/)
- Customer's audit of Plaid is weaker: annual SOC 2 executive summary on request, max once/calendar year.
- **Decision-relevant:** JPMorgan/Chase-style bilateral data access agreements flow through Plaid to Wildcat — a bank can trigger an assessment of Wildcat's data handling.

### 1.7 Derived / de-identified / aggregated data — the CRITICAL clause
- **[Q]** MSA Exhibit D: "**Plaid may use, reproduce, disclose, or otherwise exploit de-identified or anonymized Client Data (i.e., Client Data from which PII has been removed, de-identified, or anonymized) in any way in Plaid's sole discretion.**" — i.e., the express de-identified-data carve-out in Plaid's paper runs in **Plaid's** favor, over *Client* Data.
- Plaid's End User Privacy Policy claims the same right for Plaid: **[Q]** Plaid "collect[s], use[s], and share[s] data that has been aggregated, de-identified, or anonymized... for any purpose permitted under applicable law," including "to develop new products or services, to facilitate research," and de-identified/aggregated data is exempt from deletion obligations ("when Plaid has aggregated, de-identified, or anonymized data such that it cannot be reasonably reidentified"). (https://plaid.com/legal/ — End User Privacy Policy.)
- **No symmetric clause granting the CUSTOMER a right to create and freely exploit derived/de-identified data from End User Data surfaced in any public copy of the MSA/Developer Policy.** The customer-side texts instead push everything through (i) the approved use case, (ii) Express Consent scope, (iii) the no-sale/no-distribution rules. **UNVERIFIED whether a negotiated Plaid MSA typically adds a customer derived-data right — this is exactly the clause Wildcat should negotiate.** In practice many Plaid customers (underwriters, PFMs) build internal models/scores from Plaid data; the defensible reading is that *internal* derivation in service of the approved use case + consent is fine, and *externalizing* raw or re-identifiable data is not. Truly de-identified/aggregated output is contractually gray: Plaid's ban runs on "End User Data" and "Output," and whether a derived metric is still "Output"/"End User Data" once transformed is undefined in the public text. **SPECULATION:** Plaid is unlikely to pursue a customer over genuinely aggregated, non-reidentifiable metrics tied to a disclosed use case, but the paper gives Plaid the ability to do so.

### 1.8 Prohibition list & FCRA wall
- Developer Policy prohibited uses include: unlawful/deceptive/fraudulent purposes; no selling/renting End User Data to marketers or any third party; no storing bank credentials beyond what the Services require. (https://plaid.com/developer-policy/)
- **[Q]** MSA (Signal exhibit): "The Signal Service and any Signal Output **may not be used in whole or in part as a factor in determining an End User's eligibility for credit, insurance, employment or any other permissible purpose under the FCRA**." Non-FCRA Plaid products carry similar no-FCRA-use language; FCRA-permissible cash-flow underwriting is walled off into **Plaid Check** (see §6).
- Enforcement mechanics: Plaid "may immediately suspend or terminate access without notice" for violations; Plaid "investigates misuse... including violations of their Developer Policy"; Plaid technically enforces consent scoping ("apps can only use the data types that both the consumer and Plaid have approved, and apps can't retroactively pull unapproved data"). (https://plaid.com/developer-policy/; https://plaid.com/blog/open-finance-trust-security/; https://plaid.com/docs/link/data-transparency-messaging-migration-guide/)
- **No public enforcement precedent** of Plaid suing/terminating a customer for building a "shadow dataset" was found (searched; nothing surfaced). The known litigation is *against* Plaid (In re Plaid Inc. Privacy Litig., N.D. Cal., **$58M settlement, final approval 2022-07-20**, with injunctive relief: data minimization, deletion, Plaid Portal notice — https://www.lieffcabraser.com/2022/07/final-approval-granted-to-58-million-settlement-in-plaid-consumer-privacy-lawsuit/; https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-approves-settlement-ordering-plaid-to-pay-58-million-for-selling-consumer-data/). That settlement is why Plaid's consent/minimization tooling (Plaid Portal, Data Transparency Messaging) is now strict. **SPECULATION:** enforcement risk to a mid-size B2B customer is realistically triggered by (a) an FI complaint, (b) press coverage, or (c) an end-user complaint — not proactive audits.

## 2. End-user consent framework — CONTRACT TEXT (+ gaps for business end users)

- **Plaid End User Services Agreement (US)** (eff. 2019-07-01; customer-hosted copy: https://www.etoro.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Plaid-End-User-Services-Agreement.pdf): **[Q]** the end user "grant[s] Plaid the right, power, and authority to act on your behalf to access and transmit your personal and financial information from the relevant financial institution" — an agency-style grant. The developer's app + Plaid Link capture this consent.
- **End User Privacy Policy** (https://plaid.com/legal/): categories collected = account info, account-owner identity, balances, transactions, credentials, device info (see also https://support-my.plaid.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410324477847). Plaid may be **controller or processor depending on product**; "Unless otherwise specified in the product description, Plaid is the controller." Retention: Plaid may retain after deletion requests "as permitted or required by applicable law."
- **Plaid Portal (my.plaid.com):** end users can view connections, disconnect apps, and delete data from Plaid's systems (https://support-my.plaid.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410328321303; https://my.plaid.com/). Portal deletion deletes from **Plaid's** systems; deletion from the *developer's* systems requires a request to the developer (which the flow-down terms then make mandatory — §1.4). Privacy Request Form: https://my.plaid.com/data-subject-request-form.
- **Express Consent standard (contractual):** **[Q]** "Express Consents will be **clear and conspicuous** and will generally **specify the categories of End User Data that Client will receive and how Client will use, store and otherwise process it**, in addition to any other required disclosures under applicable laws." (Plaid MSA.) Data Transparency Messaging (Link) technically enforces category-level consent (https://plaid.com/docs/link/data-transparency-messaging-migration-guide/).
- **2026-04-16 change:** Plaid updated its consumer-facing Terms of Use to describe a direct Plaid–consumer relationship ("Plaid Monitoring Service," Plaid Web-App) — Plaid increasingly holds data on its own behalf, not only as the app's service provider (tracked diff: https://conductatlas.com/change/2026-04-16-plaid-plaid-terms-of-use-490/).
- **Business/corporate end users:** Plaid's framework is drafted around a "you" who controls the account. It handles entities only via authority representations: **[Q]** "If you are agreeing... on behalf of an organization or entity, you represent that you are authorized to do so." **The consent framework does not otherwise specially contemplate corporate account holders**; there is no entity-level consent artifact (no board-resolution mechanism, no multi-signer consent). For an LLC, the valid consenting party is a person with **actual or apparent authority** to bind the company and to access the account (member/manager/authorized signer under the bank's signature card and the LLC's operating agreement) — standard agency law, not Plaid-specific (background: https://www.upcounsel.com/authorized-signers-on-business-bank-accounts). **Plaid Portal is consumer-oriented; whether a business end user can manage/delete a business Item via Portal is UNVERIFIED** (help center is silent: https://support-my.plaid.com/hc/en-us).
- **Practical consequence for Wildcat:** consent risk concentrates in (i) making sure the human who runs Plaid Link for a borrower LLC actually has authority (an onboarding rep/warranty + officer certificate solves this), and (ii) drafting the Express Consent to cover *retention, derivation, and named categories of onward sharing* — Plaid's paper allows exactly what the consent covers, no more.

Timeline (all dates verified against cited sources):
- **2024-10-22**: CFPB finalizes the Personal Financial Data Rights rule (12 CFR Part 1033), published Nov 2024; largest data providers' first compliance date **2026-04-01** (originally); ~$850M-asset small depositories exempt as data providers. (https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/cfpb-seeks-comments-and-data-on-revised-open-banking-rule; https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-08-27-cfpb-reconsiders-section-1033-rule-signaling-potential-overhaul-of-personal-financial-data-rights-framework)
- **2025-10-29**: Judge **Danny Reeves** (E.D. (https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2025/12/cfpb-signals-issuance-of-interim-final-rules-on-section-1071-and-section-1033-amid-funding-constraints/; https://www.americanbanker.com/news/cfpb-to-issue-interim-final-rule-on-1033-open-banking)
- **2026-04-01**: first compliance date passes with no enforcement (enjoined/stayed). (https://blog.bankerscompliance.com/en/section-1033-compliance-date-further-delayed; https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2026/section-1033-compliance-date-open-banking-rule-enjoined-and-under-reconsideration)

### Does 1033 cover business/corporate accounts? — SETTLED (rule text)
**No.** Covered products = **Regulation E "accounts"** (consumer asset accounts established primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, incl. prepaid/wallets), **Regulation Z credit cards**, and payment facilitation therefrom. A business/corporate deposit account is not a Reg E account; the statutory beneficiary is a "consumer." Small-business accounts are outside the rule; the SBA size standard appears only to exempt small *banks* (< $850M assets) as data providers. (https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-10-31-cfpb-finalizes-section-1033-rule-on-personal-financial-data-rights; https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2024/1033-open-banking-cfpb-final-rule-reg-alert.html; https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13117). Adjacent: small-business *lending* data is the separate §1071 regime (itself being amended/IFR'd in 2025–26 — https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/11/20/section-1071-rule-proposed-amendments-a-deeper-dive/). Sole-proprietor accounts used primarily for personal purposes can straddle the line (**SPECULATION** for edge cases). NY's proposed state act is notable precisely because it **would** cover small-business data (cite above).

### What the (enjoined) rule would have meant for an integrator's data practices
Subpart D (12 CFR 1033.421, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1033/subpart-D/section-1033.421) — authorized third parties must:
- limit collection/use/**retention** to what is "**reasonably necessary** to provide the consumer's requested product or service";
- **no secondary use**: targeted advertising, cross-selling, and **sale of covered data** are deemed NOT reasonably necessary (i.e., banned absent separate authorization);
- **reauthorize annually** (max 12-month authorization); on **revocation**, stop collecting and stop using/retaining previously collected data unless still reasonably necessary for the requested service; revocation must be as easy as authorization, no fee. (https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f8c14f48-fdeb-4b60-92c0-53e151e434ac; https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/10/The-CFPB-Final-Rule-on-Personal-Financial-Data-Rights-What-Financial-Institutions-Should-Know)
- Screen scraping: the rule pushed the industry to developer interfaces (no credential-based access via the interface); with the rule enjoined, scraping's demise is being driven **contractually** by banks (see §4), not by regulation. (https://akoya.com/blog/combating-the-risks-of-screen-scraping-a-guide-for-financial-institutions)

**For Wildcat:** because borrowers are businesses, the 1033 regime — in any version — is mostly **irrelevant as a compliance burden AND unavailable as a data-access right**. Wildcat cannot invoke 1033 to force a bank to serve business-account data; access rests entirely on Plaid's bilateral agreements with banks. The 1033 secondary-use/retention/revocation architecture is still the best template for what "market-standard" consent hygiene looks like (and what NY-style state laws may impose on small-business data).

- **Business-account data specifically:** bilateral agreements are private; no public evidence surfaced that banks carve business accounts *out* of aggregator agreements, but 1033 never required business-account APIs, so **coverage of business/commercial accounts in bank APIs is purely voluntary/contractual — UNVERIFIED per-bank** and a genuine operational risk for Wildcat (a bank could cut or price business-account endpoints without regulatory constraint). Fee pass-through risk: Plaid said "no changes at this time" (Sept 2025) — pricing risk to Wildcat is forward-looking, not current.

## 5. GLBA, state privacy, confidentiality duties — SETTLED LAW (mostly favorable to Wildcat)

- **GLBA/Reg P protects "consumers" = natural persons obtaining financial products "primarily for personal, family, or household purposes."** Business-purpose account data is **not NPI**; commercial clients (incl. sole proprietorships acting for business purposes) are outside the Privacy Rule: "does not apply to information about individuals... that you collect only for business purposes." (https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/manuals-guides/federal-consumer-financial-protection-guide/compliance-management/deposit-regulations/privacy-consumer-financial-information-regulation-p; https://www.federalreserve.gov/regulations/cg/reg_p_cg.pdf) **Nuance:** a sole proprietor (or an owner's personal account connected by mistake) obtaining a product for personal/family/household purposes IS a consumer — intake controls should screen out personal accounts. Note the GLBA **Safeguards Rule** can still attach to Wildcat as a "financial institution" (FTC jurisdiction) if it is significantly engaged in financial activities, and Plaid's flow-downs contractually require GLBA-grade security regardless (Plaid RFI response, https://downloads.regulations.gov/CFPB-2023-0052-0917/attachment_1.pdf).
- **CCPA/CPRA:** the employee and **B2B exemptions expired 2023-01-01** — personal information of business contacts/signers of California companies is fully covered (access/deletion/opt-out rights for the *individuals*, not the LLC). (https://katten.com/california-consumer-privacy-acts-employee-and-b2b-exemptions-to-expire-on-january-1-2023; https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/10/california-consumer-privacy-act-employee-and-b2b-exemptions-expire-january-1-2023) GLBA-covered data is exempt from CCPA, but business-account data is NOT GLBA-covered — so CCPA is the operative regime for CA individuals appearing in Wildcat's dataset (signers, and **counterparty individuals in transaction descriptions**). Entity-level data (the LLC's balances) has no CCPA rights.
- **Bank confidentiality duties:** US common law implies a bank duty of confidentiality to its customer (e.g., Peterson v. Idaho First Nat'l Bank line of cases); UK: Tournier v. National Provincial (1924). These bind the *bank*, and are waived by the customer's consent (Plaid's EUSA agency grant). They reach Wildcat only **through contract chains** (bank↔Plaid data access agreements → Plaid↔Wildcat flow-downs → audit rights in §1.6). **UNVERIFIED:** no Delaware/NY statute creating a general bank-secrecy right for corporate customers was identified; NY's relevant instrument is the *proposed* Financial Data Rights Act (§3) plus 23 NYCRR 500 (cybersecurity) if Wildcat becomes a NYDFS-regulated entity (**SPECULATION** — not currently applicable to an unlicensed analytics holder).

## 6. FCRA — when does Wildcat's model become "consumer reporting"? — SETTLED LAW at the core, gray at edges

- **Core rule:** a "consumer report" is information by a CRA bearing on a *consumer's* (natural person's) creditworthiness etc., used/expected to be used for FCRA-permissible purposes (credit for personal/family/household use, employment, insurance...). **Reports on businesses, used for commercial credit decisions about the business, are outside FCRA.** Commercial credit reporting (D&B, Experian Business, Equifax Commercial) is federally unregulated as such — the FTC polices it only via UCC §5 deception authority (2022 D&B consent order: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/04/federal-trade-commission-finalizes-order-against-dun-bradstreet-deceiving-businesses-failing-update; FTC 2023 small-business credit-reporting inquiry: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-launches-inquiry-small-business-credit-reports; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_credit_reporting). CFPB has flagged SMB report accuracy but has no FCRA hook (https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/inaccurate-small-business-credit-reports-can-block-access-to-loans/).
- **The personal-guarantee / sole-prop trap:** FTC's Tatelbaum letter (2000-07-26) first said no permissible purpose to pull a *consumer* report for a commercial application (https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/advisory-opinions/advisory-opinion-tatelbaum-07-26-00); the 2001 agency/FTC exchange settled that where the individual **is or will be personally liable** (sole proprietor, co-signer, guarantor), the lender has a §604(a)(3)(A) permissible purpose (OCC AL 2001-6: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/advisory-letters/2001/advisory-letter-2001-6.pdf; https://www.nacmcommercialservices.org/accessing-consumer-reports-on-business-owners-are-you-following-fcra-604-for-permissible-purposes-for-obtaining-a-consumer-report/). Symmetrically: **if Wildcat assembles data bearing on an INDIVIDUAL (a guarantor's personal cash flows, an owner's personal account) and furnishes it to lenders for credit decisions, Wildcat is acting as a CRA** — registration-free but strict-liability-laden (accuracy, disputes, permissible-purpose certification, adverse-action flow).
- **Plaid's own architecture proves the point:** Plaid put FCRA-purpose cash-flow data into a separate CRA subsidiary, **Plaid Check** (Consumer Report product, launched 2024), precisely so that lenders can use bank data in *consumer* credit decisions without the aggregator becoming an unwitting CRA; all other Plaid products carry contractual no-FCRA-use clauses (§1.8). (https://plaid.com/blog/introducing-plaid-check-cra-consumer-report/; https://fintechtakes.com/articles/2023-11-06/plaid-officially-jumps-into-lending/; https://plaid.com/docs/check/)
- **Wildcat's safe harbor:** report only on **entities**, for **commercial credit** to those entities, and (a) exclude personal accounts at intake, (b) if lenders demand guarantor analytics, either stay out of it or accept CRA status deliberately (or route through an existing CRA like Plaid Check/Experian). **[SPECULATION]** A DeFi wrinkle: if "lenders" on Wildcat markets include natural persons extending credit for personal investment, FCRA analysis still keys on the *subject* of the report (the borrower business), so this shouldn't flip the analysis, but sole-prop borrowers would.

## 7. Precedents — permissioned-data aggregators reselling analytics — CONTRACT TEXT / market practice

- **Dun & Bradstreet model:** collect trade/payment data on businesses (largely without the subject's consent), sell reports — legally possible only because commercial credit reporting is unregulated (§6). FTC's 2022 order constrains deception, not the model.
- **Enigma:** buys/licenses **anonymized card-panel data from a consortium of issuer banks** ("750M+ anonymized cards," "$4.5T annual spend") and sells merchant-level revenue signals — the legal mechanism is **bank-side aggregation/anonymization before Enigma touches it**, plus entity-level (not person-level) output. (https://enigma.com/resources/blog/introducing-merchant-transaction-signals; https://documentation.enigma.com/getting_started/enigma_data) This is the cleanest precedent for "sell business-level metrics derived from bank data."
- **Heron Data (ToS):** **[Q]** customers "own all right, title and interest in their customer data, while the company **retains ownership of any data that is based on or derived from the customer data**." (https://www.herondata.io/terms) — i.e., a vendor-side derived-data ownership grab is market-standard in SMB-underwriting analytics. Wildcat should expect its own analytics vendors to attempt the same clause **against Wildcat** and strike it.
- **Validis** (lender/audit workflows over permissioned accounting data): markets "data is permissioned, traceable and tied back to source," read-only, "never used to train AI" (https://www.validis.com/) — i.e., the audit-workflow segment sells *restraint*, not data exploitation.
- **Middesk** builds from public/government sources (SOS registries, 400+ sources), not permissioned bank data — different legal base (public records) (https://www.middesk.com/).
- **Ocrolus / Ntropy / Codat / Boss Insights / ForwardAI:** processor-style positioning (analyze the lender's/platform's permissioned data; derived-data clauses vary). **UNVERIFIED in detail** — their negotiated terms are not public. Ocrolus is notable as one of only three Plaid-integrated Asset Report auditors (with Fannie/Freddie) (https://plaid.com/docs/assets/).
- **Pattern across precedents:** nobody with a durable model resells *identifiable third-party-sourced bank data*. The three viable structures are (1) subject-consented reporting (CRA-style, or commercial reports on entities), (2) aggregated/anonymized panels licensed from the data's original holders (Enigma), (3) processor-with-derived-data-rights (Heron). Wildcat's model is a hybrid of (1) and (3).

## 8. The specific Wildcat re-sharing pattern — analysis against Plaid's paper

Pattern: Wildcat (Plaid customer) holds raw borrower bank data → grants scoped access to analytics third parties (DeFi Llama dashboards, auditors) → only **derived metrics** reach lenders.

- **Is third-party sharing allowed?** Yes, structurally — Plaid's own documents contemplate it, but through exactly two doors: (1) **Permitted Service Providers** (Wildcat's contractors/service providers acting *on Wildcat's behalf*), and (2) disclosure to other third parties — both requiring **End User Express Consent** and (for downstream data recipients like "Secondary Investors" in the lending flow-downs) **written flow-down agreements** with Wildcat strictly liable (§1.3). Independent redistribution ("sell, resell, license, distribute... any Services or Output") is banned **absent Plaid's written agreement** (§1.3). The safest reading: analytics providers MUST be papered as Wildcat's Permitted Service Providers (service agreements: process only for Wildcat, no independent use, deletion, security, flow-down of Plaid restrictions), NOT as independent data recipients; lenders receiving only derived metrics should still be treated as flow-down recipients if what they receive is recognizably "Plaid-Provided Data."
- **Consent language needed (per MSA Express Consent standard + 1033-style hygiene):** clear and conspicuous; names the data categories pulled; states retention period/criteria; states that Wildcat will (a) analyze the data to produce credit metrics, (b) share raw data with named categories of service providers (analytics, auditors) bound to confidentiality, and (c) publish/share derived, entity-level metrics with lenders and publicly (if DeFi Llama dashboards are public, SAY SO — public disclosure of even derived borrower metrics is far beyond a normal consent's scope); signed by a person representing authority to bind the entity. Re-consent on material scope change; honor revocation and deletion (except as law requires retention).
- **DeFi Llama specifically:** a public dashboard is **not** a "service provider" relationship; if borrower-level metrics are public, this needs explicit, named consent from each borrower and a defensible position that the metrics are not re-identifiable "End User Data." **[SPECULATION]** This is the single most Plaid-policy-exposed element of the design; per-borrower published metrics with borrower consent is contractually survivable, but Plaid could still object under the no-distribution clause if metrics are deemed "Output."
- **Contrast — Plaid-intermediated sharing (Relay / Audit Copy / Check sharing):** Plaid offers first-party rails where the *recipient* is also Plaid-integrated: Asset Report **Audit Copy tokens** (auditors: Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Ocrolus only), `/credit/relay/create` **Relay tokens** to share reports with partners (e.g., Alloy), and Consumer Report OAuth sharing (https://plaid.com/docs/assets/; https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/assets/; https://plaid.com/docs/check/oauth-sharing/). Advantages: consent, scoping (e.g., 60-day vs 730-day views), and audit trail live inside Plaid; Wildcat never redistributes data itself. Disadvantage: recipients must be Plaid-integrated and Plaid-approved; DeFi Llama would not qualify today. **Recommendation-grade point:** use Plaid rails for auditor access where possible; use the Permitted-Service-Provider + Express Consent route for everything else; get Wildcat's **use case (including derived-metric publication) approved by Plaid in writing** — the MSA's "use case permitted by Plaid in writing" hook makes this both necessary and sufficient contractual cover.

## 9. UK/EU angle — SETTLED LAW (brief)

- **GDPR:** corporate financial data is not personal data, BUT business-account transaction streams are saturated with personal data — payroll lines (employee names, salaries), sole-trader counterparties, directors' names. Wildcat, deciding purposes/means for its analytics asset, would be a **controller** of that embedded personal data (Plaid itself is controller or processor "depending on the product"; default controller — https://plaid.com/legal/). Consequences: lawful basis needed (legitimate interests balancing for employee data the employees never consented to sharing — must be assessed, likely passable for credit underwriting with minimization, weak for public dashboards), data-subject rights (an employee of a borrower could demand erasure from Wildcat's dataset), international-transfer mechanics (SCCs/UK IDTA), and the derived-metrics exemption only once outputs are truly **anonymous** (GDPR Recital 26 standard is stricter than US de-identification). Minimize by stripping/tokenizing counterparty and payroll strings at ingestion.
- **PSD2 / UK open banking:** unlike US 1033, **PSD2 AIS and the UK CMA Order cover business payment accounts** (SME current accounts) — a genuine regulated access right exists for EU/UK borrowers. Access must run through an authorized **AISP** (Plaid Financial Ltd., FCA FRN 804718, authorized since 2018/2019 — https://plaid.com/blog/aisp/; https://plaid.com/blog/fca-registration-and-how-plaid-can-help/). Consent: UK re-confirmation every 90 days now sits with the TPP (FCA scrapped bank-side 90-day SCA re-auth in Nov 2021; TPP reconfirms consent — https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/41042/fcas-amendment-to-the-90-day-psd2-rule-comes-into-effect; https://gocardless.com/guides/posts/90-day-re-authentication-rule); EU runs 180-day SCA renewal (https://www.saasant.com/blog/uk-eu-open-banking-consent-feed-break-fix/). AIS data may only be used for the consented account-information service — UK/EU law imposes the purpose-limitation that US law currently doesn't.

## STRUCTURED ANSWER — "Does Plaid's contractual framework, privacy law, data portability, or borrower rights materially limit Wildcat's ability to build a proprietary data asset from permissioned corporate bank data?"

**Short answer: Yes — but almost all of the binding force is CONTRACT (Plaid's paper and the bank agreements behind it), not law. For corporate borrowers, US statute is close to empty; the real constraints are Plaid's use-case approval, Express Consent scoping, the resale ban, and end-user deletion/revocation rights that Plaid makes contractual even where law doesn't.**

### Ranked REAL constraints
1. **Plaid MSA use-case + no-resale/no-distribution clauses (CONTRACT TEXT).** Data and Output are licensed "solely" for the Plaid-approved use case; selling/licensing/distributing Output to third parties requires Plaid's written agreement; disclosure of End User Data to ANY third party (even Wildcat's own service providers) requires Express Consent. This is the #1 constraint on "proprietary data asset" ambitions — the asset is really a *license position*, terminable by Plaid, with no public precedent of enforcement but suspension-on-notice rights. Mitigation: negotiate a written use-case approval covering retention, derivation, service-provider access, and derived-metric publication; negotiate an explicit customer-side de-identified/derived-data ownership clause (Plaid wrote one for itself — Exhibit D — so the concept is in their paper).
2. **End-user deletion/revocation rights, made contractual by Plaid (CONTRACT TEXT).** "Promptly Delete any FI Data upon request by the applicable End User" — a borrower who leaves Wildcat can gut the historical raw-data asset (law-required retention excepted; genuinely anonymized aggregates survive under Plaid's own de-identification logic, but that carve-out is express only for Plaid). Design the asset so value persists in non-reidentifiable derived layers.
3. **Bank-side commercial power over business-account access (IN FLUX, trending adverse).** No statute obliges any US bank to serve business-account data; JPMorgan's paid-access regime is live (Sept–Nov 2025, >95% of pulls under paid contracts), the CFPB's re-proposal reopens the fee ban, and Wells/PNC push Akoya. Banks can reprice, degrade, or cut business endpoints at will; Plaid's costs will eventually reach customers. This is a supply/continuity risk to the dataset itself, bigger than any privacy rule.
4. **Consent-scope integrity for corporate authorization (SETTLED agency law + CONTRACT).** The whole edifice rests on the human who clicked Link having authority to bind the LLC, and on the Express Consent actually describing retention, derivation, third-party analytics access, and (if applicable) public dashboards. An overreached consent poisons everything downstream (and is the likeliest FI-complaint vector). Cheap to fix with rep/warranty + officer authority certificate + well-drafted consent.
5. **FCRA tripwires at the individual boundary (SETTLED LAW).** Entity-only reporting for commercial credit = unregulated (D&B model). Ingesting guarantor/owner personal accounts or furnishing individual-bearing metrics for credit decisions makes Wildcat a CRA (accuracy, disputes, permissible purpose). Sole-prop borrowers are the leak in the wall. Screen at intake; route guarantor analytics through an existing CRA (Plaid Check) or accept CRA status deliberately.
6. **GDPR/UK for any EU/UK borrower + CCPA for embedded individuals (SETTLED LAW, moderate).** Payroll/counterparty personal data inside business transactions makes Wildcat a controller (GDPR) and gives CA individuals CCPA rights post-B2B-exemption expiry; PSD2/UK purpose limitation binds the use of AIS data. Manageable with tokenization at ingestion and EU/UK-specific consent flows — but public per-borrower dashboards are hard to justify under GDPR.
7. **Plaid/FI audit and assessment rights (CONTRACT TEXT).** Plaid, the banks, and their regulators can demand compliance documentation and third-party audits of Wildcat and conduct technical assessments — the mechanism by which constraints 1–4 actually get enforced.

### Paper tigers (as of Aug 2026)
- **CFPB 1033** for this model: enjoined (Oct 29, 2025), unenforceable, being rewritten weaker at OIRA (Aug 6, 2026) — and it never covered business accounts anyway. Neither a burden nor a usable access right for Wildcat. Watch NY's Financial Data Rights Act (covers small-business data) as the sleeper that could convert 1033-style secondary-use/retention limits into binding state law.
- **GLBA** for corporate-account data: business-purpose data is not NPI; only intake leakage of personal/sole-prop accounts creates exposure. (Safeguards-Rule-grade security is still contractually required by Plaid.)
- **Federal regulation of commercial credit reporting**: none exists; only FTC deception authority (D&B order) — keep marketing claims about report accuracy honest.
- **Bank common-law confidentiality duties**: waived by end-user consent; reach Wildcat only via the contract chain already counted in #1/#7.
- **Plaid Portal borrower rights for business items**: consumer-oriented; entity self-service deletion rights are contractual (via #2), not portal-driven (UNVERIFIED whether Portal even handles business items).
- **Plaid enforcement history against integrators**: no public precedent of Plaid enforcement against a customer for data misuse/shadow datasets — the risk is real on paper (suspension/termination/audit) but historically dormant; do not confuse dormant with absent, since the 2022 $58M settlement pushed Plaid toward visible privacy enforcement.

**Net assessment:** Wildcat can lawfully build and keep a proprietary derived-data asset over corporate bank data — US law barely touches the entity-level model — but the asset sits on two revocable foundations: Plaid's written use-case approval (and its no-resale clause) and per-borrower consent/deletion rights. The durable, uncontested layer is **entity-level, genuinely non-reidentifiable derived metrics produced under a Plaid-approved use case with explicit borrower consent** — negotiate the derived-data clause with Plaid now, paper analytics partners as Permitted Service Providers, keep raw data and individuals' data out of everything that leaves the perimeter, and treat bank data-access fees/terms as the principal long-run threat.
