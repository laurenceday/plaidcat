Can Wildcat own the borrower data layer?

Whether Wildcat can pull permissioned corporate bank data through Plaid, keep and normalize it, expose slices of it to third-party analysts, and hand lenders only derived metrics. And whether holding that data builds a moat. Researched deep, answered plainly, with the evidence attached.

A Shoggoth Fiat run · six research streams · current as of 26 August 2026. Direct plaid.com fetches were blocked from the research environment; official claims rest on Plaid's published OpenAPI specification and indexed extracts of plaid.com, cross-checked against operator reports. Verify the decisive clauses before relying on them.

The two answers

Core question 1 · How hard is it?

Technically: modest. Commercially: cheap but conditional. Legally: contract-bound, not law-bound. The pipe is real. Plaid connects business accounts at SMB and fintech banks, tokens never expire by design, and a two-year transaction backfill plus webhook-driven monitoring is a supported, documented pattern that costs a rounding error ($6k-$12k a year at 100 borrowers). The hard parts sit at the edges: treasury portals at large banks are invisible to every aggregator, only the primary account holder can link at some banks, consent decays on 12-month timers at others, and the whole model needs Plaid's written use-case approval plus carefully drafted borrower consent to survive its contract terms. Third-party analytics access is structurable today; publishing raw data never is.

Core question 2 · Is the data a moat?

The raw data is not a moat. The outcomes dataset is. Any competitor with the borrower's consent re-pulls the same 24 months of history in roughly one API call, and borrowers hold contractual deletion rights over raw data. What compounds instead: history past month 24 that no re-link can reproduce, the standing consent graph with contractual teeth, and above all the joining of cash-flow features to onchain credit outcomes. Nobody else can assemble that label set. Build the layer as a label factory from day one and the moat builds itself.

The desired architecture, annotated

The brief, restated

Wildcat is onchain credit infrastructure. The economically important facts about a corporate borrower live off-chain: balances, inflows and outflows, customer concentration, recurring obligations, liquidity, working-capital behavior, statements, payment performance. The premise under test is that Wildcat should hold and control access to a permissioned dataset of those facts, let third parties such as DeFi Llama, accountants, or auditors compute agreed metrics against defined subsets, and distribute only the agreed derived results to lenders. Raw transaction-level information stays confidential.

"Wildcat has obtained this information with the borrower's permission. DeFi Llama is granted permissioned access to a defined subset of that information for the purpose of calculating agreed financial metrics. Only agreed derived results may be distributed to lenders."
