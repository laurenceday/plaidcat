Part III · Commercial coverage

Business accounts: where the money hides

Consumer aggregation does not translate to corporate treasury. This is the highest-risk assumption in the whole design, so it gets the bluntest page: what connects, what never will, and what to do about it.

1 · The two front doors

Plaid's business support is real and officially marketed: Transactions for Business (May 2025) claims 95% of US banks that serve small businesses, with business accounts connectable at 4,500-plus institutions. official The catch is which door that covers. Plaid reaches the online-banking front door: chase.com business profiles, Bank of America small business, wellsfargo.com business, and the fintech banks. It does not reach the treasury back door: JPMorgan Access and Chase Connect, CashPro, Wells Fargo Vantage, CitiDirect, SinglePoint, PINACLE. Those are separate authentication realms with no Plaid institution entries, and no other aggregator reaches them either. operator-reported

3 · Failure modes

Wrong front door

The company banks at JPM or BofA commercial, so the institution is literally absent from Link's search. Nothing to debug; the account is out of scope.

Entitlement lockout

The CFO or bookkeeper connects fine to the bank and sees zero linkable accounts: at BofA, documented policy says only the primary owner's login can share business accounts, and operators report the pattern elsewhere. The treasury team almost never holds that credential. Onboarding must name the right human.

Separate-institution confusion

Citibank against CitiBusiness Online, Cross River Personal against Business. The borrower picks the wrong entry and the login fails or the wrong accounts appear.

Consent decay and login churn

OAuth consent-refresh timers at eleven US institutions (mostly 12 months; Brex every 3); password resets and security interstitials elsewhere. Items die on schedule and by accident; a dead Item during a drawdown is a monitoring outage. Watch the warning webhooks and drive re-auth 30 days early.

Partial account selection

OAuth pickers let the borrower share a subset and hide the rest. Reconcile the shared account count against the borrower's declared accounts, or the dataset silently measures a fraction of the business.

Scraping sunset

The majors are all API-based now and the long-tail portals that never got APIs simply rot. What SMB lenders actually run: Plaid or Finicity for banking, accounting-platform data alongside, manual document upload as the universal floor. operator-reported

5 · Alternatives and complements

  • Finicity (Mastercard). The long-tail business fallback; historically steadier on odd business portals, lists CitiBusiness Online as a distinct connection. operator-reported
  • MX, Akoya, Teller. MX for regional depth (also cannot reach WF commercial); Akoya is the bank-owned pipe now under PNC-to-Plaid traffic; Teller is developer-friendly with a smaller institution set.
  • Codat and accounting data. For corporate borrowers, accounting-platform data (QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite) may be the better primitive than bank rails; Codat's edge is exactly that. Complement, not replacement.
  • Direct treasury APIs and file feeds. CashPro API, JPM Access API, CitiConnect, or borrower-configured BAI2 / ISO 20022 / SFTP feeds. They reach exactly what aggregators cannot, at the cost of borrower-driven paperwork with their bank. For large cooperative borrowers this is a real path, not a fantasy.

6 · The three risks and their mitigations

Risk 1 · The treasury-portal wall

The bigger the borrower, the less any aggregator sees; the borrowers with the largest credit lines have the least visible cash. Mitigate three ways: make borrower-provisioned direct bank feeds a first-class integration path; contractually require a monitorable operating account that reference flows transit; accept auditor-style monthly statement upload with tamper checks as the covenant-grade floor.

Risk 2 · Entitlement and consent decay

Links that never form (sub-user lockouts, partial account selection) and links that silently die (12-month windows, password churn). Mitigate with an onboarding runbook that puts the credential owner through Link and verifies shared-account count against declared accounts; monitor consent expiry and drive update-mode re-auth 30 days early; write re-linking obligations with cure periods into the loan agreement.

Risk 3 · Single-aggregator dependence

Product gaps, per-institution health volatility, and the new pay-for-data regime all concentrate in one vendor. Mitigate with multi-aggregator routing on a pre-flight check of the products array and health status; gate features per institution instead of assuming uniform capability; keep the manual statement pipeline alive so no borrower is unsupportable.
