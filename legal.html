Data rights & law

Who owns what, and what actually binds

The binding constraints on Wildcat's data ambitions are contract, not law. Plaid's full standard MSA is public because a customer filed it with the SEC, so this page works from actual clause text. Not legal advice; re-verify quotes against live pages before relying on them.

1 · Ownership under Plaid's paper

Three clauses do most of the work. All quoted from the standard Plaid MSA as filed on SEC EDGAR (Live Oak Financial Form 1-A exhibit) and Plaid's Developer Policy. official

The end user owns the raw data

Plaid "reserves and retains all right, title, and interest in the Services and related Output (except for raw End User data, which belongs to the End User)." Wildcat owns neither the raw data nor the Output. It holds a revocable license position.

Use is welded to the approved use case

Output "must [be] use[d] solely in their Client Application for the use case permitted by Plaid in writing." Anything beyond that use case needs Plaid's written approval, independent of what the borrower consented to.

No resale, no unconsented disclosure

Clients "cannot sell, resell, license, sublicense, distribute, rent or lease any Services or Output to any third party" absent written agreement, and will not "disclose, transfer, syndicate or distribute End User Data to any third party (including its Permitted Service Providers)" without the end user's Express Consent.

Deletion on request is contractual

The client "will promptly Delete any FI Data upon request by the applicable End User," law-required retention excepted, and Plaid's docs separately instruct deleting data on revocation, citing the 1033 rule's reasonably-necessary standard. Borrower deletion rights exist here even where no statute grants them.

The one-sided carve-out

The MSA's de-identified-data clause runs only in Plaid's favor: "Plaid may use, reproduce, disclose, or otherwise exploit de-identified or anonymized Client Data ... in any way in Plaid's sole discretion." No symmetric customer right appears anywhere in the public text. Whether a derived metric stops being "Output" once transformed is undefined. This is Wildcat's number-one negotiation item: get an explicit customer-side derived-data and de-identified-data right written into the order form, alongside written approval of the full use case including derived-metric publication. official

Two more facts worth carrying. Plaid and the banks hold audit rights over Wildcat: documentation demands, Plaid-approved third-party audits, and technical assessments by Plaid or a financial institution, at most yearly unless law requires more. And there is no public precedent of Plaid enforcing against a customer for building a retained dataset; the known litigation ran against Plaid itself (the $58M privacy settlement of 2022, which is why its consent tooling is now strict). Dormant is not absent. inferred

2 · The re-sharing pattern, tested

The brief's pattern: Wildcat holds raw data, analysts get scoped access, lenders get derived metrics only. Plaid's own paper contemplates onward sharing through exactly two doors, and the pattern fits through them.

  • Analysts as service providers. DeFi Llama, an accounting firm, or a ratings boutique gets papered as Wildcat's Permitted Service Provider: processes only for Wildcat, no independent use, deletion duties, security terms, Plaid restrictions flowed down. Their access still requires the borrower's Express Consent naming the category of recipient.
  • Lenders as flow-down recipients. Where what lenders receive is recognizably Plaid-sourced, Plaid's End Client Flow Down Terms apply: written agreements, use restrictions, Wildcat strictly liable. Genuinely derived, entity-level metrics argue for a lighter touch, but the paper is safest read broadly.
  • Consent drafting. The MSA's standard: clear and conspicuous, naming the data categories received and how they will be used, stored and processed. For this model the consent must also name retention, derivation, the recipient categories, and any publication of derived metrics. If dashboards will be public, say so in the consent, in those words.
  • Who consents for an LLC. Plaid's framework handles entities only through an authority representation. The valid consenting party is a person with authority to bind the company and access the account. An onboarding representation plus an officer's certificate closes the gap; it is also the likeliest complaint vector if skipped.

Why Wildcat mostly does not care

1033 covers consumer asset accounts under Regulation E and credit cards under Regulation Z. Corporate deposit accounts were never in it. The rule is neither a compliance burden nor an access right for this model: Wildcat cannot invoke it to force a bank to serve business-account data, and access rests entirely on Plaid's bilateral bank agreements. Two things still matter. The rule's hygiene architecture (no secondary use without authorization, 12-month reauthorization, easy revocation) is the market's template for what good consent looks like. And New York's proposed Financial Data Rights Act would cover small-business data with penalties up to $10,000 per violation: the sleeper to watch. official

The adjacent weather is bank economics. JPMorgan's paid data-access regime went live through 2025 and covers over 95% of its pulls; Wells Fargo and PNC steer fintechs toward bank-owned Akoya; the revised federal proposal reopens the fee question. No statute obliges any US bank to serve business-account data at all. Supply continuity, not privacy law, is the long-run threat to the dataset. official

4 · GLBA, FCRA, CCPA, GDPR

GLBA

Protects natural persons' personal-purpose data. Business-purpose account data is not nonpublic personal information. Two residues: screen out sole-prop and personal accounts at intake, and expect Safeguards-grade security anyway because Plaid's flow-downs require it.

FCRA

Entity-only reporting for commercial credit is federally unregulated; that is the Dun & Bradstreet model. The tripwire is individuals: furnish data bearing on a guarantor or sole proprietor for credit decisions and Wildcat becomes a consumer reporting agency with accuracy, dispute and permissible-purpose duties. Plaid built Plaid Check precisely to absorb that role. Report on entities; route guarantor analytics through an existing CRA or accept CRA status deliberately.

CCPA

The B2B exemption expired in 2023. California individuals inside the dataset, signers and the people named in transaction descriptors, hold access and deletion rights. The LLC itself holds none. Tokenize names at ingestion and the problem shrinks.

GDPR / UK

Business transaction streams are saturated with personal data: payroll lines, sole-trader counterparties, directors. For EU/UK borrowers Wildcat is a controller of that embedded data, needs a lawful basis, and faces erasure requests from people who never consented. PSD2 covers SME accounts and imposes purpose limitation; public per-borrower dashboards are hard to justify. Strip and tokenize person-identifying strings at ingestion.

5 · How others structure it

  • Dun & Bradstreet: consentless commercial reporting, possible only because entity-level reporting is unregulated. FTC polices deception, not the model.
  • Enigma: buys anonymized card-panel data aggregated on the bank side, sells merchant-level revenue signals. The cleanest precedent for selling business-level metrics derived from bank data: anonymize before it moves, output only entity-level.
  • Heron Data's terms: the vendor "retains ownership of any data that is based on or derived from the customer data." Expect Wildcat's own analytics vendors to try that clause on Wildcat; strike it. official
  • Validis: sells restraint (permissioned, traceable, never trains models) to the lender-audit segment. A viable posture for winning conservative counterparties.

6 · Real constraints, ranked, against paper tigers

Paper tigers: 1033 in any version (enjoined, consumer-only, being rewritten weaker); GLBA for corporate data; federal regulation of commercial credit reporting (none exists); bank common-law confidentiality (waived by consent, reaches Wildcat only through the contracts already counted); and Plaid's enforcement history against integrators (none public, which is not the same as none possible).
